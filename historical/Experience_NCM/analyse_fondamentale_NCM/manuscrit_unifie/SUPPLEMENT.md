# Supplementary material

# Study dependencies, data and historical selection {#app-design}

This supplement documents the completed campaigns; it does not report new experiments. The archive uses eight stable aliases: `base` (initial fundamental analysis), `mechanistic` (level-5 and revision follow-up), `generalization` (cross-level and text-tokenizer audit), `repair` (encoding-pool modification), `temporal` (stops, omissions and readout), `oracle` (correct-answer codec control), `crossed` (conditional compensation and text generator), and `terminal` (terminal table and certificate). `evidence/campaigns.json` resolves them, and `evidence/manifests/` retains their historical manifests. The original reports, protocols and journals are copied with the source code rather than edited to agree with the present narrative.

The original 2,000 puzzles per Sudoku difficulty were checked for format, clue consistency and valid reference solutions. Solution counting found all official puzzles unique. They share 2,000 solution families across difficulties, so separate checkpoint analyses do not create three independent sets of families. The hard split and later choices are retained exactly. Training-only preprocessing and validation selection applied to the fitted probes; their bootstraps did not refit them. Later transformations and interventions on the old test partition are explicitly exploratory. Two new sets were generated by randomized backtracking followed by greedy clue removal, after separate local freezes: 256 for the crossed test and 1,024 for terminal readout. Both have 30 clues. MRV backtracking and Algorithm X independently checked uniqueness. Exclusions use digit-normalized D4 keys against the known prior families and newly retained families; this is not a full Sudoku-isomorphism audit or a test of unknown training contamination.

The new data do not constitute an externally administered replication. The same research process developed, executed, checked and interpreted the campaigns. The terminal direction was explicitly sought as a possible positive result after earlier non-confirmations. Old validation already showed 201/400 certified candidates and 208/256 successful pilot writes before the local 30% coverage threshold was frozen. Those choices must remain visible even though the subsequent fixed rule was evaluated on new families. No archive can establish the absence of every unrecorded analytical choice. The terminal editorial revision corrected interpretation without changing the scientific observations or historical protocol.

No new statistical result is computed by the manuscript tools. Values printed through numerical placeholders come from `evidence/numbers.json`, which supplies the saved JSON pointer, formatting rule and source hash. Intervals with different historical seeds are not silently pooled: in particular, the main Sudoku reversal comparison uses the common `oracle/audit.json` summaries. Whole-number design constants and algebra are editorial text; the ledger separately links the underlying design records.

# Pooling identity, its boundaries and the repair {#app-pooling}

For adaptive area pooling from $L=89$ to $K=9$, window $j\in\{0,\ldots,8\}$ covers the zero-based half-open range
$$
W_j=[\lfloor 89j/9\rfloor,\lceil89(j+1)/9\rceil).
$$
The intervals are $[0,10)$, $[9,20)$, $[19,30)$, $[29,40)$, $[39,50)$, $[49,60)$, $[59,70)$, $[69,80)$ and $[79,89)$. With eight row separators in the serialized grid, each window contains one whole row. The first and last have one separator, the others two. Every valid row contains each digit exactly once, so the embedding sum depends on the fixed multiset rather than its ordering. The positional sum is also fixed. The proof does not assume that token embeddings are equal, learned in a particular way, or well separated.

This identity is before the native GroupNorm and residual subtractions. GroupNorm uses input-dependent statistics, and the quantizer processes a residual after previous levels. The raw identity alone cannot force the final quantizer input or nearest code to be constant. `pooling_algebra.json` records the eight/nine/ten-position controls; `level5_diagnostic.json` follows the intermediate signals and codebook. The constant hard identifier 11 is a checkpoint observation, not a theorem about all NCMs. Training-mode dropout controls on frozen weights and positive EMA counts are not longitudinal training records and cannot establish why the optimizer learned this checkpoint.

Three interventions have different meanings. Bypassing the level-5 prediction with the observed fixed identifier preserves its residual and matches recorded native outputs on the 600 test and 256 external puzzles. Removing that residual changes some answers even when success counts do not change. Removing several levels changes more of the generative input distribution and has no established non-inferiority guarantee. The historical reduced-prediction configuration gives 510/600 exact grids versus 516/600 native, with nine failures introduced and three corrected. These results are retained in `generalization/sudoku_test.json`; they do not justify an exact or performance-equivalent nine-level model. A zero bootstrap interval after no changed success statuses is degenerate, not a population equivalence result.

For weighted pooling, $\sum_iw_{ji}=1$ because $\sum_it_{ji}=0$. At $\alpha=1$ all weights are nonnegative; $\alpha=2$ can give negative weights and is only diagnostic. For a swap of tokens $a,b$ at $i,h$, linearity gives
$$
P_w(x')-P_w(x)=(w_{ji}-w_{jh})(e(b)-e(a)).
$$
This is nonzero if both factors are nonzero. Other collisions can still exist, especially because dimension reduction is retained. A fixed additive bias independent of the input cannot add between-input variance. Likewise, a permutation-invariant maximum is not in general an order-sensitive remedy. The historical operator variants, sham and ordering controls remain in the repair records; validation selected the fixed weighting before its held-out comparisons.

For completeness, native and repaired exact solution reconstructions are 600/600 and 600/600 on hard test; 256/256 and 256/256 externally; 600/600 and 600/600 on easy; 599/600 and 599/600 on medium. The one medium error is not erased by rounded token accuracy. All these inputs are reference solutions. Digit permutations, transposition and latent noise are codec tests, not robustness to incorrect puzzle clues. Noise at 0.05, 0.2 and 0.5 times the training RMS, five seeds on 128 solutions, leaves both codecs at 100% exact reconstruction; it changes codes but establishes no reconstruction-robustness advantage. A post-hoc single-level replacement control distinguishes changing level 5 alone from fully re-encoding all levels. Its motivation and results remain in `repair/compensation.json` and the archived amendment.

LM1B and TinyStories native-tokenizer audits use separately described random and long-text strata (which can overlap), with non-PAD reconstruction accuracies 99.86% and 99.98% on the random strata. Neither reproduces a constant native level. Raw within-block permutation controls preserve an additive mean at the relevant length, whereas contextualization and the full quantizer can still change codes. LM1B generator sizes sharing a tokenizer do not supply independent tokenizer replications. These controls delimit the Sudoku collapse claim rather than prove that every text level is useful.

# Temporal observables and oracle controls {#app-temporal}

Let $A_i$ indicate that cell $i$ is wrong at 8 and correct at 9. Let $B_i$ indicate that it is wrong at least once at 10, 11 or 12. The reversal statistic is $\sum_i A_iB_i/\sum_iA_i$. Bootstrap resampling groups numerator and denominator by source puzzle. Initially empty-cell summaries and all-cell summaries are both saved; the latter are the primary reported numbers. The denominator is a selected set of corrected cells, so matching oracle and generated rates is not matching identical events.

On hard test, native cell accuracies at levels 8--12 are 45.31%, 61.51%, 75.93%, 81.68% and 98.10%. All preterminal complete grids fail, even when scored on the 81 digits separately from the separators. The oracle also has zero exact grids before the final level and exact final reconstruction. This does not prove the latent contains no earlier answer accessible to a different reader. Intermediate representations are not required to be complete Sudoku candidates. Moreover, the configuration includes fine-level dropout, so an explanation that the decoder was only trained on full twelve-level representations would be unjustified.

The recorded temporal omission computes the targeted prediction but suppresses its residual, then regenerates later contributions. Omitting 9 on 128 old test puzzles gives 103 exact grids versus 105 natively; the success difference is -1.56 percentage points, 95% CI [-3.91, 0.00]. It changes 16 complete outputs and 1962 codes. This implementation does not save that prediction's compute. Contributions differ in norm across levels; their omissions are not a perfectly dose-matched importance ranking. The level-12 omission destroys reconstruction but does not establish a phase transition at 12.

Other temporal diagnostics are retained without upgrading their evidential status. Fractional readout at 21 points on $z_8+\gamma r_9$ holds the native residual fixed and measures how the decoder crosses readout boundaries; it is not a family of native systems for finite-size scaling. Resume tests at $\gamma=0,0.5,1,1.5$ separate omission, interpolation and extrapolation. The native sham reproduces codes and outputs. Before/after-9 Gaussian susceptibility uses 64 sources, two doses and three paired seeds with absolute norms matched to $z_8$. It is a restricted sensitivity comparison, not a proof of global attractors or irreversibility. Zero success-status changes give degenerate intervals, not equivalence.

The original raw hard-all count of 41,620 wrong-to-correct events at level 9 is over 2,000 puzzles and 81 cells, not a number of decisions or independent puzzles. Some simultaneous changes are degradations. Counts also depend on the number of codes added at each level. Twelve unequal stages do not provide a system-size family or a phase-transition control parameter. The old descriptive bend fits are retained in the temporal records and not interpreted as evidence of a singularity.

# LM1B generation and reconstruction {#app-text}

The text-generation campaign executes the published LM1B small generator (97,065,600 parameters) at a pinned checkpoint revision, with its file digest checked against the repository LFS entry. Native eager generation uses 128 distinct seeds, batch size one, a CLS prefix (identifier 101), first-code sampling from the saved unigram, temperature 1, top-k 0, top-p 1 and CFG 0. The lengths are $1,8,16,24,32,40,52,64,76,92,108,128$; the ninth stage is not the same resolution as Sudoku's. Intermediate accumulations are copied before later in-place additions to avoid aliasing. The final accumulation agrees with the native generation path, and the first two seeds and their twelve raw readouts were replayed.

The primary readout is the decoder's raw argmax at all 128 fixed positions. Official frequency/adjacency postprocessing is only used for final inspection and differs from raw at 14.37% of positions; it is not mixed into the trajectories. The secondary non-special mask uses final identifiers 104--30521 and therefore conditions on the future. A trajectory, not a token treated as independent, is the resampling unit. Adoption at 11 or 12 has no later abandonment by construction under the final anchor. The entire transition series is retained, rather than selecting a favorable peak.

An initial text loader left stale references to shared bias views after parameter assignment. Its 46 trajectories were excluded, retained as invalid historical outputs and never entered into the reported aggregates. The corrected loader copies into the shared heads, compares saved tensors with the checkpoint, and reconstructs non-persistent rotary buffers for meta/mmap loading. The corrected run restarts the same seeds and sampling settings. These engineering checks support fidelity within their tested scope; they are not extra independent research replications. Loader structure and assets are described in `crossed/lm1b_checkpoint_structure.json` and `crossed/lm1b_asset.json`.

A random-tail control adds to $Y_{10}$ a Gaussian direction of the same norm as $\Delta T$. It matches amplitude but not codebook or continuation geometry. # Terminal table, intervention support and certificate {#app-terminal}

For each of 16 terminal identifiers, the table counts digits in native outputs at the corresponding 81 cell positions on 1,000 old training puzzles, then takes the modal digit. Writing chooses, for each digit, the identifier with the largest training count of that digit. No reference solution is used to fit either table. Development validation and the 32-source pilot are retained as development, not final evidence. The pilot's terminal-residual-only result was originally zero exact 89-token sequences; post-hoc format auditing found 10/32 with all 81 digits correct but zero with all eight separators correct, and 96.18% reference-digit accuracy. The original zero is not evidence that terminal contributions can never reconstruct an entire grid's digits.

The final intervention samples one initially empty target per source before observing the intervention effects, and requests all digits except the native readout. This produces eight correlated requests per source. The main metric excludes requests for the already-native digit, though those sham requests are saved. Of 240 failed writes, a post-hoc description finds 237 unchanged target cells and three different, unrequested digits. This does not locate a context dependence in the decoder rather than neighboring or earlier latent information.

Changing a terminal code propagates through an affine residual path with a width-three convolution. The controls match each changed position's L2 norm. FP32 subtraction also produced tiny tails outside the mathematical three-position support (maximum per-position norm $6.28\times10^{-6}$), and the main controls include those tails. A post-hoc direct-affine FP64 calculation, converted to FP32 for decoding, leaves all 1,152 requested complete readouts identical to the original computation in both arms. This audit is numerical validation, not a replacement of the frozen experiment. No other-cell argmax damage was observed for target substitutions, but logits and separators were not the collateral metric. Zero affected sources among 128 gives a descriptive Wilson upper bound of approximately 2.91% at 95%; 1,024 requests are not 1,024 independent sources.

The candidate policy uses generated codes, the table and the puzzle only. It checks digit ranges, 27 unit constraints, clues and formatting. Uniqueness is a property independently established for these data, not a free certificate supplied by the running policy. Under uniqueness and sound verification, acceptance implies the reference solution. Exact native fallback supplies the pointwise non-degradation argument. The policy cannot guarantee correctness for rejected candidates, and a valid alternative solution on a non-unique puzzle can differ from a designated reference.

Hard coverage is 552/1024; all accepted grids are correct on these data. Native/hybrid exact counts are 883/885. The two improvements do not establish a strict population advantage. On the first 256 common fresh families, easy covers 66/256 (25.78%) and medium 99/256 (38.67%), with native/hybrid counts 106/107 and 188/189. Tables are fitted separately for distinct weights. These are the common 30-clue puzzles, not official easy and medium evaluation distributions. There is no text replication of this table or editing policy.

The benchmark includes the first 64 new sources, regardless of acceptance; 59.38% are certified. Four warm-up puzzles are excluded. Three paired pipeline measurements per source randomize AB/BA and puzzle order, and readout-only measurement uses 16 internal repetitions. The historical session ran without other campaign inference. The saved paired bootstrap first averages repetition log-ratios per source. A geometric ratio can differ greatly from the ratio of arithmetic means because accepted and fallback branches have different costs. Neither the descriptive arithmetic reduction nor a readout geometric ratio supplies a confirmed whole-model speedup. One session does not quantify machine-to-machine or session-to-session variation.

# Other outcomes retained from the search {#app-secondary}

The four-part narrative does not replace the other completed analyses (E14). The initial announced hard level-6 confidence contrast gives an AUROC gain of 0.0183 for adding hidden states to the baseline, 95% paired CI [-0.008, 0.042]. It does not establish added value for that fitted procedure. It also does not prove hidden states contain no relevant information. The baseline uses puzzle, distributional and decoded-validity observations; the latter are post-hoc instrumentation, not a matched deployment-cost comparison. At the final stage a validity checker can characterize success on these unique puzzles without native self-monitoring.

The initial naked-single probe decodes an initial constraint from final activations (AUROC 0.877 versus 0.496 with shuffled training labels); a direct candidate-count computation solves that target by definition. Decodability does not establish use, a unique circuit or a human Sudoku algorithm. Removing a fitted confidence direction improves exact success by 0.50 percentage points, within the 0.00--0.67 range of five norm-matched random directions; it does not establish metacognitive specificity.

Revision probes were also examined before the later attribution tests. The conditional objective gives 55.97% direction accuracy versus 55.65% baseline on changed cells, a 0.33-point difference with interval [-0.10, 0.76]; an old-external contrast is -0.07 points with interval [-0.66, 0.51]. Earlier probe variants and steering controls are retained in the mechanistic records, not silently replaced by a preferred formulation. Candidate choice, changed-cell conditioning, decoder access and the final reference are distinct sources of information; an improvement on a selected probe objective would still not identify native control. Other original controls, including deterministic repeats, batch effects, transformations and checkpoint/corpus transfer, remain available in their complete JSON summaries.

# Evidence, source code and document verification {#app-evidence}

`EVIDENCE_MAP.md` and `evidence/claims.json` give E01--E14, the relevant records and code paths, and an explicit limit for every central claim. `evidence/numbers.json` links numerical placeholders to their exact saved JSON pointers. `evidence/copied_files.json` links the organized copies back to their historical paths and SHA-256 values. Compact NPZ files are built by copying existing NPY members byte for byte; `evidence/traces.json` lists each retained member and the source archive hash. No arrays are transformed to assemble this view. Large hidden-state arrays, checkpoints and some secondary traces remain in the historical folders, identified by their manifests. The compact supplement is not a claim that every historical run can be executed from small files alone.

`code/history/` preserves campaign scripts and documents. `code/models/` preserves the CPU implementation and intact upstream source. Dependencies and environment snapshots are included; libraries are not falsely represented as campaign-authored code. Historical runners are evidence and can launch experiments in their original environment; the unified `run.sh` only assembles documents and checks artifacts, and never invokes them. Replaying a historical model is not part of this task.

The previous manifests are compared before and after synthesis. Document checks verify hashes, pointers, numerical substitution, links and PDF compilation. They do not rerun inference, fit probes or recompute uncertainty. Historical verification counts include per-puzzle software assertions, not thousands of independent replications. The new document checks are likewise not peer review, a proof against p-hacking, or independent validation of the research conclusions. The archive retains limitations and negative outcomes along with favorable observations.

The manuscript is a research draft, not a submitted or accepted paper. AI assistance was used in the research workflow, code, audits and drafting; same-agent checks are not independent oversight. Human authorship, responsibility for the work, affiliations, funding, conflicts and any required disclosure must be established before a submission. No such metadata or external review is invented here.
